Over 120 people showed up at Student BASE on High Tech Campus last week to talk about something most of us have felt but rarely hear said out loud: international talent keeps leaving Brainport. The Career Compass Netherlands event on June 12 brought together students, graduates, professionals, and employers to go through the honest realities — the visa maze, the Dutch workplace culture that surprises a lot of newcomers with how low-key it is, the loneliness of a first job search in a country where you don't know the rules.

Dr Navid Mohamadi of SHEXON put the core problem plainly: "We all know ASML and Philips. But how many of us know smaller-sized companies?" That's the gap the evening was really about — not whether Brainport is a good place to build a career, but whether people can actually find their way in beyond the two or three names everyone already knows. Rabobank economist Marc Cootjans added that Eindhoven and Amsterdam are the only two Dutch regions where economic growth has been double the national average for a decade, and that 10,000 new homes are planned near the station. The question he left hanging: can this region become somewhere people want to stay, not just work?

I find that question more interesting than any of the growth numbers. The infrastructure is there. The jobs exist. What's still missing, at least for a lot of internationals I talk to, is a clear path in and a reason to stick around.

---

The day after that event, the campus itself opened its doors. The HTCE Open Day on June 13 was the usual mix of cleanroom tours, VR demos, kids' activities, and companies like Philips, NXP, Signify, and Axelera AI showing what they actually do. But the part I'd highlight for anyone newer to the region: the Internationals Fair at HTC 1e. Holland Expat Center South, Expat Spouses Initiative, Expat Housing Center, Expat Mortgages South, ABN Amro, and language school Regina Coeli were all there in one place. If you've been trying to figure out which organisations actually help expats with practical things, that kind of room doesn't come together very often.

---

Earlier in June, the Fe+male Tech Heroes Conference at the campus took on a different kind of gap. Researchers, clinicians, founders, and investors spent June 3 working through a number that stopped me when I first read it: endometriosis affects 1 in 7 women and has received €44M in startup funding. Erectile dysfunction startups have received €1 billion. FemTechNL argued the Netherlands is positioned to lead on women's health technology, and named five campus-based startups already building in that space — Certain, Salvia BioElectronics, Sirius Medical, VivArt-X, and Vi Health. After the conference, Invest NL named women's health a top priority for 2026. Two of the panelists had co-authored a piece in *The Lancet* calling it a "societal imperative."

For anyone who assumed the campus was mainly about chips and semiconductors — this is worth knowing. The community here is broader than it looks from the outside.

---

A few weeks earlier, on May 21, the AI Innovation Center hosted AI Meetup XL — sold out, over 300 people. The topic was European AI sovereignty. Speakers from Axelera AI, Euclyd, and Datacation pushed back against the idea that Europe has already lost the AI race. The real problems they named were fragmented funding, slow adoption, and a tendency to regulate before scaling. Bernardo Kastrup of Euclyd said: "Europe can be strategically and largely autonomous before 2030. It just requires will and courage." Recordings of all the sessions are on the AI Innovation Center website if you want to go deeper.

---

The Summer Tent is also open on campus now for informal lunches and meetups — worth knowing if you're looking for a lower-stakes way to spend time at the campus without needing a reason to be there.

---

One thing I keep thinking about after the Career Compass event: the region clearly wants international talent to stay, and the support organisations, the events, the housing pipeline are all real. But I'm curious what actually makes the difference for the people who do stay long-term. Is it finding the right job, the right community, or something harder to name? If you've been here a while, I'd be interested in what you'd tell someone who just arrived.
