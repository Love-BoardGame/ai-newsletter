**"We Have a Problem": 38 Nationalities Gathered at HTCE to Talk About Why International Talent Keeps Leaving Brainport**
On June 12, over 120 students, graduates, professionals, and employers gathered at Student BASE on High Tech Campus Eindhoven for Career Compass Netherlands — an event designed to close the gap between international talent and the smaller companies in the region that desperately need them. The evening tackled the honest realities of building a career as a newcomer: the visa maze, Dutch workplace culture (less "salesy" than many expats expect), the loneliness of a first job search, and how to stand out when everyone has the same degree. Speaker and moderator Dr Navid Mohamadi of SHEXON named the problem plainly: "We all know ASML and Philips. But how many of us know smaller-sized companies?" Rabobank economist Marc Cootjans told the room that Eindhoven and Amsterdam are the only two Dutch regions where economic growth has been double the national average for a decade — and that 10,000 new homes are planned near the station area — but that the real question is whether the region can become a place people want to *stay* in, not just work in.

**The Campus Opens Its Doors — and Has a Dedicated Fair Just for Expats**
On June 13, High Tech Campus Eindhoven held its annual Open Day (11:00–16:00, free entry), with Philips, NXP, Signify, Shimano, Axelera AI, imec, and SMART Photonics among the companies opening their doors for demos, cleanroom tours, VR experiences, and kids' activities. A standout feature for newcomers: the Internationals Fair at HTC 1e, a dedicated space with booths from Holland Expat Center South, Expat Spouses Initiative, Expat Housing Center, Expat Mortgages South, ABN Amro, and language school Regina Coeli — all in one place, with games and prizes. This is one of the best opportunities of the year for anyone new to the region to get a feel for the campus, meet the support organisations in person, and understand the scale and variety of what goes on here.

**FemTech Meets Brainport: A Sold-Out Conference Put Women's Health — and Funding Gaps — on the Table**
On June 3, the Fe+male Tech Heroes Conference brought together researchers, clinicians, startup founders, and investors at HTCE to confront a stark reality: women spend 25% of their lives in worse health than men, yet endometriosis (affecting 1 in 7 women) has received only €44M in startup funding compared to €1 billion for erectile dysfunction startups. Dutch FemTech organisation FemTechNL declared that the Netherlands is well placed to lead this field, pointing to five campus-based startups — Certain, Salvia BioElectronics, Sirius Medical, VivArt-X, and Vi Health — already building women's health solutions. Following the conference, Invest NL named women's health a top priority for 2026, partly driven by a co-authored article in *The Lancet* by two panelists calling it a "societal imperative."
📎 ['Once you see it, you can't unsee it' – Fe+male Tech Heroes Conference highlights](https://hightechcampus.com/news/once-you-see-it-you-cant-unsee-it-highlights-from-female-tech-heroes-conference-championing-womens-health/) · HTCE · June 2026
💡 *Why it matters: For expats — especially women new to the region — this shows the campus has an active community working on inclusion and healthcare, not just chips and semiconductors.*

**300 People on Campus Asked: Can Europe Be AI-Sovereign Before 2030?**
On May 21, the AI Innovation Center at HTCE hosted AI Meetup XL, a sold-out evening with over 300 attendees debating European AI sovereignty. Speakers from Axelera AI (AI chips), Euclyd (AI infrastructure), and Datacation (AI applications) argued that Europe hasn't lost the AI race — but that fragmented funding, slow adoption, and regulation-before-scale are its real enemies. The headline line came from Bernardo Kastrup of Euclyd: "Europe can be strategically and largely autonomous before 2030. It just requires will and courage." Recordings of all sessions are freely available on the AI Innovation Center website.
